[intrigued] Pac-Man wasn't called Pac-Man, and the reason is one letter. And it's real. In 1980, Toru Iwatani created a game at Namco in Japan. He wanted a game everyone would enjoy, not just war games. He called it Puck-Man. The name came from paku, the Japanese word for chomping. In May 1980, Puck-Man hit Tokyo arcades. But here's the problem. An American company, Midway, wanted to bring it to the U.S. They warned Namco about the name. Kids could scratch the P and turn it into an F. [dramatic] In other words, one bored kid with a key could ruin every machine. So Puck-Man became Pac-Man. It launched in America that October and exploded.
