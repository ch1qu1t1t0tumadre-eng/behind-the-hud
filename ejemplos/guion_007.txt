[intrigued] The first famous video game easter egg was a programmer's secret revenge. And it's real. In 1980, Warren Robinett made Adventure for the Atari 2600. It sold about a million copies. But Atari had a rule. Programmers got no credit. No names on the box. Nowhere. Atari didn't want star programmers asking for more money. So Robinett hid his name inside the game. A one-pixel dot, carried into a secret room, revealed one line. Created by Warren Robinett. [dramatic] In other words, he smuggled his signature past his own bosses. By the time a fifteen-year-old player found it and wrote to Atari, Robinett had already quit. At Atari, they called it an easter egg, and the name stuck. Today, almost every big game hides one.
